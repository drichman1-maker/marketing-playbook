# Iron Stake

## What it is

Lease-to-purchase listing marketplace (rent to own and lease option) with
every material term disclosed on the listing. Sellers pay $249 a year
($125 for the first 40), verified against title. Buyers pay nothing and
contact sellers directly. First markets: NC and GA vacation areas, then
Colorado. Stripe is in test mode and launch is gated on the
owner checks in `iron-stake/docs/launch-status-2026-09-15.md`. A subsidiary
of ACHV Capital LLC.

## The real problem is supply, not awareness

A marketplace with sample homes cannot be marketed to buyers. The only
marketing that matters until there are ten real approved listings in one
metro is getting sellers to list. Everything buyer-facing waits.

## Target markets: NC and GA vacation areas first, then Colorado

Owner's call, and a good one. Vacation markets have a seller the big
platforms ignore: the second-home or short-term-rental owner who wants out
at their price but will not get it from a conventional sale this year.

Likely first areas (confirm against your own knowledge of the markets):

- **NC mountains:** Asheville and Black Mountain, Boone and Blowing Rock,
  Highlands and Cashiers, Bryson City and Maggie Valley.
- **NC coast:** Outer Banks, Wilmington and Carolina Beach, Oak Island,
  Emerald Isle.
- **GA mountains:** Blue Ridge, Ellijay, Helen, Dahlonega, Lake Lanier.
- **GA coast:** St. Simons, Tybee, Jekyll.

Why this seller fits a lease option:

- Short-term rental revenue has softened and several of these towns have
  tightened STR rules. Owners who bought at 2021 and 2022 prices cannot sell
  without a loss and cannot cover the carry. A lease option lets them lock
  the price they need and have a tenant-buyer cover the mortgage.
- Many owners are out of state. They respond to email, text and a clear
  written process better than to a local agent's phone call.
- The buyer side is real too: people who want a mountain or beach home and
  cannot qualify for a second-home mortgage today.

Legal note for counsel, not for marketing copy: North Carolina has a
specific statute for option-to-purchase contracts executed with lease
agreements (General Statutes Chapter 47G) with recording and disclosure
requirements, and separate rules for contracts for deed (47H). Georgia has
less specific statute and more case law. Have counsel confirm the NC
agreement template meets 47G before the first NC listing is approved.

Colorado second, same seller profile in the mountain towns, once NC and GA
have ten listings and the process is proven.

## Seller acquisition, zero budget

Who lists a lease-option home: tired landlords, owners who could not sell
at their price, FSBO sellers, small investors who already do lease options
by hand, and agents whose listings expired.

- **Your own licence and network first.** Past clients, the brokerage,
  lenders and title companies you already know. Ask every one: "Who do you
  know with a rental they are tired of, or a listing that did not sell?"
  This is free and warm.
- **Expired and withdrawn listings** in the target towns. If you hold or
  can get MLS access in NC or GA through a referral agent, pull these. If
  not, Zillow and Realtor.com show price-cut history and days on market,
  which finds the same owners. A letter or email: "Your home did not sell at
  $X. Here is a way to get your price from a buyer who needs two years to
  qualify."
- **Short-term rental owners.** The single best list in a vacation market.
  Airbnb and VRBO listings with high availability and recent price drops,
  owners in the town's STR host Facebook group, and anyone who posted about
  the new STR rules. Message them directly. They are the first 40.
- **Vacation rental property managers.** They know which owners are
  bleeding. The $40 referral reward already exists; make these managers the
  referral channel and consider a higher reward for the first ten.
- **Existing rent-to-own posters.** People already posting "rent to own"
  on Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace and Zillow rentals in these towns are
  doing the deal with no verification and no structure.
- **Local REIA and landlord meetups** in Asheville, Wilmington, Gainesville
  GA and north Atlanta. Go, do not pitch, offer the rent credit calculator
  and the agreement walkthrough as free tools.
- **Build in public.** The TAM report, pricing methodology and walkthroughs
  in `ironstake-sites` are unusually good. Publish the non-confidential
  parts as articles. "Why lease-option deals are unmeasured" and "What
  Furnished Finder charges and why we charge $50 more" are the kind of
  posts investors share.

Target: 50 seller conversations in December, ten approved listings by
mid-January across NC and GA. Then Colorado.

## Buyer side, later

Only after ten real listings:

- The area Facebook groups (every one of these towns has a large "living
  in" or "moving to" group), r/FirstTimeHomeBuyer and r/realestateinvesting,
  with the honest framing of what lease options are and are not.
- The rent credit calculator as a standalone SEO page. "Rent to own
  calculator" is a real query with weak competition.
- Lenders and credit repair counsellors, who meet buyers who are two years
  from qualifying every day.

## Cross-pollination with the real estate business

Buyers who cannot qualify today become conventional buyers in two to five
years. Sellers who list on Iron Stake may want a conventional listing later.
Keep both lists in one CRM with a source tag, and make sure your agent
disclosures cover the relationship.

The owner checks and counsel sign-off in the launch status doc. Marketing a
marketplace that is still in Stripe test mode burns the first impression.
